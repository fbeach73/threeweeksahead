Recovery Gear

The short list of thingsthat actually helped.

Nobody hands you a packing list for open heart surgery. This is mine.

None of this is fancy and none of it is medical. It's the stuff that made the first month at home easier, sorted by when you'll want it. If you can, get the "before surgery" items lined up ahead of time. You will not feel like shopping afterward.

Your hospital may give you some of these (mine sent me home with a spirometer). Ask before you buy.

  • 01
    Before surgery
    A firm cough pillow

    The single best tip in the guide. Brace it hard against your chest before every cough, sneeze or laugh. A heart-shaped sternum pillow is made for exactly this, but any firm pillow you can grip with both hands works.

    Before surgery
    A wedge pillow

    You will not be able to lie flat for a while, and you can't push yourself up with your arms. A wedge keeps you propped without a stack of pillows sliding around at 3 a.m.

  • 03
    Before surgery
    A shower chair

    A sit-down shower became my favourite thing in the world. Fifteen minutes to sit, get clean, breathe and take stock. Standing that long in week one is not happening.

  • 04
    Before surgery
    Shirts that button or zip in front

    For the first few weeks you can't raise your arms over your head. A couple of button-front shirts and a zip hoodie mean you can get dressed without help or pain.

    Before surgery
    Slip-on shoes

    Bending over to tie laces is off the table. Slip-ons let you get out the door for the walks that are doing the actual healing.

    First month home
    A bathroom scale

    Sudden weight gain of a few pounds in a day or two can mean fluid, and that's a call-the-doctor sign. Weigh yourself every morning and write it down.

    Four new medications, twice a day, in a brain that's still foggy. This is how you stop asking yourself whether you already took them.

    First month home
    Compression socks

    If they harvested a vein from your leg, it swells. Light compression socks made the walks more comfortable and the evenings less puffy.

    See it on Amazon →
  • 11
    First month home
    Ice packs and a heating pad

    The soreness isn't only the incision. It's the trauma of the surgery itself, plus the way you have to sleep while the breastbone heals, which leaves muscles aching in places you didn't expect. Ice on the chest plate or wherever it's sore, heat on the muscles. Having both within reach of the bed made the nights easier.

    See it on Amazon →
  • 12
    First month home
    A small space heater or an electric blanket

    My body temperature was all over the place for weeks. Whether it was the healing or the new medications or both, I'd go from fine to chilled in minutes. A little heater by the chair, or an electric blanket, takes the chill off fast without heating the whole house.
